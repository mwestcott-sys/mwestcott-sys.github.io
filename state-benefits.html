Your State's Veteran Benefits

These benefits don't come from the VA — they come from your state, and every state writes its own rules. There's no federal floor, and nothing carries over when you move. Here's what to look for, and exactly where to go check it for yourself.

Why This Page Looks Different

We Can't Catalog 50 Rulebooks — But We Can Point You to Them

Every state runs its own veterans affairs department, writes its own eligibility rules, and can change them at its next legislative session. A page (or a book) claiming to list the exact current benefit in all 50 states would be wrong within a year, no matter how carefully it was researched today.

So instead of guessing at details that go stale, this page does two things: it tells you what categories of benefit to look for, and it links you straight to your state's own official department — the only source that's actually current.

Mistakes We See Most Often

Before You Assume Anything

  • ✕ Assuming a state benefit transfers with you when you move. It doesn't — you start over under an entirely new set of rules every time you cross a state line.
  • ✕ Assuming your federal VA disability rating automatically triggers a state property tax exemption. In most states it doesn't — you need a separate state application, often certified through a county Veteran Service Officer.
  • ✕ Assuming every state taxes military retirement pay and VA disability compensation the same way. The variation is real, and disability compensation itself is functionally untaxed almost everywhere.
  • ✕ Not realizing a surviving spouse or Gold Star family member may qualify under their own distinct category, not simply by inheriting the veteran's eligibility.
  • ✕ Treating an outdated source, or another state's program, as current without checking your own state's official department directly.
